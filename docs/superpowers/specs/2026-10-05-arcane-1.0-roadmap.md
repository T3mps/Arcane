**Date:** 2026-10-05. **Status:** decisions recorded (section 6); the space game is defined (section 2.0); the slices are re-scoped (section 4). **Target:** "AAA-capable for our needs" by the end of January 2027; **February is an acceptable slip** (user, 2026-10-05).

This document fixes the finish line and orders the work. Every arc spec from here on cites the row of section 2 it advances. An arc that advances none is out of scope for 1.0.

## 2. Definition of done (1.0)

### 2.0 The binding principle: one engine, two games

**(user, 2026-10-05)** Arcane is ONE engine for both planned games. Nobody ever maintains two engine versions, and nothing may stop Aphelyon from taking engine upgrades.

- **Aphelyon:** an F2P gacha RPG with an isometric overworld and Wakfu-style in-place turn-based combat. It is the **first game shipped on Arcane**: at 1.0 the user's team starts building it, and its revenue funds the space game.
  - **1.0 bar:** Aphelyon is **fully developable with minimal engine changes after 1.0**. The team works through the C++ game module plus editor and data-driven authoring (no scripting language or visual scripting requested).
  - **Platform:** PC (Steam) at launch.
- **The space game:**
  - a first-person looter-shooter (Destiny-style frames + attachments + rarity traits; hitscan or projectile by frame), with Rust-style full-loot death;
  - snap-piece bases on asteroids that are procedurally generated each season and raidable any time;
  - a full spherical planet and seamless arcade-assisted ship flight to orbit (seats only for the MVP);
  - a respeccable skill-tree class system;
  - layered dynamic PvE;
  - seasons (a black-hole reset) with meta progression;
  - stylized art (Deadlock / Borderlands);
  - **one solar system per nation on a dynamic server mesh: 1-2k players, designed for 200-300 per fight.**

  **1.0 bar:** Arcane can produce a **high-quality demo** of it, and its heavy features (dynamic meshing, the full planet, ...) **layer on later WITHOUT fundamental or architectural engine changes**. 1.0 therefore ships the SEAMS (rows D17-D20), even where full implementations follow 1.0.

Each row's bar is what the 1.0 audit checks. "Exists" is not enough; the bar is the measurable part.

### Already at bar (no 1.0 arc needed)

- **Editor shell.** Docking, the inspector with ownership rules, the outliner, the asset browser and pages, undo/redo, the shader and material graphs, the node framework, the Hub and the project format, and crash and hang capture with a reporter window.
- **Build and hot reload.** The game as a DLL, arcbuild (MSBuild and Ninja), hot reload, and the ABI gate.
- **RHI.** NRI over D3D12 and Vulkan, the render graph, bindless resources, the GPU scene, GPU mesh culling, GPU crash diagnostics and breadcrumbs.
- **ECS.** Astra with the visitor and remap work, and the Identity GUID model.
- **2D physics.** Manifold2D, with Box2D-v3 parity and multithreading parity proven.
- **Assets.** Cook, mesh import (cgltf + meshoptimizer) and texture cook (BC7).
- **Automation.** Headless hosts, the Servitor witness, and golden-image gates.

## 4. The vertical slices (D16)

The slices are the integration tests. They force the systems to meet before the 1.0 audit.

- **Slice A, Aphelyon (the 1.0 gate):**
  - an iso overworld region, an encounter in Wakfu-style in-place combat with real characters and animations, a gacha pull flow, game UI end to end, and save/load through the persistence seam;
  - **built the way the team will build it:** game module + editor + data files.
  - **Gate:** a team member (not the engine author) can add a character and a banner without engine changes.
- **Slice B, the space-game demo:**
  - land on a demo cube-sphere planet in first person;
  - fight PvE, loot a frame with attachments;
  - die (full-loot drop);
  - fly a ship seamlessly to orbit and a seeded asteroid;
  - place a few snap pieces;
  - with 2 server processes handing the player and the ship across a zone boundary, and 2+ players connected.
  - **Gates:** the D1 perf budget holds; a 30-minute soak with no desync or crash; a cold start reaches gameplay in under 10 s from a cooked build.

## 5. Risks and how they are handled

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **The user is the bottleneck** (spec approvals, desk checks, decision forks) | Specs are written one arc ahead. Forks are batched into one sitting per arc as multiple choice with a recommended pick. Desk checks are replaced by automated golden, witness and perf gates wherever possible. |
| **Judgement-heavy arcs** (render look, animation feel) | Every such arc ships a reference scene plus goldens. For the look, the user approves captures, not code. |
| **Integration debt** | The vertical slice starts mid-December. Two weeks of hardening are reserved in January. |
| **Agent pipeline throughput** (CLI quotas, capacity, incidents; four on 2026-10-04) | Runner hardening is done (done-marker waits, no self-review, the one-turn guard, capacity backoff with a model fallback, Sonnet runner). Spare capacity comes from the Sonnet side lanes and from cloud credits ($250, expiring 2026-11-05) for Linux and CI work. |
| **Replication** is the classic long pole | It starts in November in its own lane, with the soak test as its gate from day one. |
| **Machine contention** (one desk, GPU tests serialize) | GPU-heavy arcs stay on lane A; lanes B and C stay CPU-bound where possible. |
